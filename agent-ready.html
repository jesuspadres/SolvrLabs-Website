What’s Changing

Search is turning into delegation

Customers used to scroll ten blue links and call around. More and more, they ask an AI and let it do the legwork.

AI gives the recommendation

“Who’s a good roofer in Mesa?” now gets a short answer with two or three names — not a page of results. If you’re not one of those names, you don’t get the call.

Agents take the action

AI agents can now open websites, compare options, fill out forms and book appointments for the person who asked. Your website’s visitor might not be a person at all.

They pick what they can read

If an AI can’t confirm your hours, service area, prices or how to book, it moves on to a competitor it can confirm. Being the best plumber doesn’t help if the AI can’t tell.

What I Fix

Six things that make you agent-friendly

Most of this is invisible to human visitors. It’s the layer underneath your site that AI reads to decide whether to trust you.

Structured business data

Behind-the-scenes markup (schema.org) that spells out who you are, what you do, where you work, your hours and your reviews — in the format AI and search engines read directly.

An AI briefing file (llms.txt)

A plain-text summary of your business written for AI: services, pricing, service area, how to book. Like a cheat sheet so the AI describes you accurately instead of guessing.

AI crawler access

Plenty of sites accidentally block the bots behind ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity, or hide key info in images and scripts they can’t read. I find and fix those blind spots — on your terms.

Agent-friendly booking & quotes

Clear, labeled forms and direct booking links an agent can actually complete — instead of “call us for a quote.” Still easy for humans, now usable by their assistants too.

Consistent listings

AI cross-checks your Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp and your site. When your name, phone, hours or service area don’t match, it trusts you less. I line them all up.

Direct agent connections Advanced

For businesses ready to go further: a secure connection (API or MCP server) that lets AI assistants check availability and book jobs straight into your calendar or job software.

Because that’s where a growing share of customers start. When someone asks an AI “who should I call?” it names a couple of businesses — not twenty. When they say “just book it,” the agent books whoever it can actually book. Being agent-friendly means:

Agent-friendly doesn’t mean wide open. Everything I set up has guardrails:

  • Nothing private is exposed. AI only sees what’s already public — services, hours, area, price ranges. No customer data, ever.
  • Spam and fake bookings are filtered. Forms keep spam protection, submissions are validated, and repeat or suspicious requests get rate-limited.
  • You stay in control of bookings. Requests can require your approval, a confirmed phone or email, or a deposit before they hit the schedule.
  • Bad bots get blocked. Known AI assistants are allowed; scrapers and unverified bots can be throttled or shut out.
  • Direct connections use keys and limits. Advanced API/MCP access is permissioned, logged and can be switched off in one step.
